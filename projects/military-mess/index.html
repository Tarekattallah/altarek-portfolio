Military Mess & Warehouse Management System

A comprehensive management system translating real-world military logistics into structured software business logic.

Backend Logic Inventory Modeling Data Allocation

Overview

The Military Mess & Warehouse Management System is designed to support the complex operations surrounding military mess halls. It handles everything from food supply inventory and meal planning to tracking stock consumption and predicting reorder logic based on daily reservations.

The Problem

Military logistics require extreme accuracy. Managing inventory manually or via spreadsheets leads to food waste, expiration issues, and inaccurate consumption forecasting. The system needed a way to automate inventory deductions based on specific meal reservations while strictly adhering to expiry-based stock rotation.

The Solution

I architected a backend solution heavily focused on transforming real-world business rules into maintainable software operations. By utilizing robust inventory algorithms, the system automatically pulls from warehouse stock logically and warns administrators of impending expiry or low stock.

Key Features & Business Logic

  • Meal Management & Reservations: Handled daily meal planning linked to headcount reservations.
  • Inventory & Stock Tracking: Accurate tracking of all incoming and outgoing warehouse items.
  • FEFO/FIFO Allocation: Implemented First-Expired, First-Out (FEFO) logic to prioritize the consumption of items nearing expiration, severely reducing food waste.
  • Consumption Calculations: Automated deduction of raw ingredients based on predefined meal recipes and reservation counts.
  • Notifications & Dashboards: Alert systems for reorder logic when stock hits minimum thresholds.

Engineering Challenges

The most significant challenge was the Allocation Engine. When a meal is reserved, the system doesn't just deduct generic "rice" or "meat". It must iterate through warehouse batches, find the batches with the earliest expiry dates, and deduct the exact quantities from those specific batches across multiple lots. This required writing complex, transaction-safe database logic to prevent race conditions during concurrent updates.

Lessons Learned

This project was a deep dive into domain-driven design. It taught me how to sit with domain experts (military logistics personnel), understand their manual constraints, and accurately model those constraints into database schemas and scalable backend services.
